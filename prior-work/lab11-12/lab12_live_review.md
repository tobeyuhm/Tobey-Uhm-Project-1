## Joby Aviation — presentation summary

1. **Input:** Commercialization revenue path.
2. **Evidence boundary:** Certification, aircraft production, routes, utilization, fares, and contribution margin remain research priorities.
3. **Model link:** Base revenue is $75M / $300M / $800M / $1,800M / $3,500M in 2026–30 and flows into operating profit and FCFE before funding.
4. **Base result:** 2030 operating profit is $375.1M; FCFE before funding is $121.2M. Per-share sensitivity value is unavailable because a terminal value is not defensible during an externally funded transition.
5. **Sensitivity:** Revenue at 30% lower/higher produces operating profit of −$202.4M/$952.6M and FCFE of −$377.5M/$577.5M. Gross-margin lower/higher produces operating profit of $25.1M/$725.1M and FCFE of −$155.3M/$397.7M.
6. **Interpretation:** Revenue-path FCFE spans $955.0M versus $553.0M for margin. All statement, cash-floor, and restored-base checks passed; this does not resolve valuation.

### Person B reviews Joby

**Person B — selection/evidence:** Why do you think Joby can reach $3.5 billion in revenue by 2030 when it is still building its air-taxi business?

**Person A:** That number is a scenario assumption, not company guidance. My model assumes certification, aircraft production, routes, customer demand, and utilization all improve over time. I would lower the forecast if certification is delayed, production is slower, or riders do not use the service as expected.

**Person B — model/valuation:** How does a delay in certification affect your value?

**Person A:** It pushes revenue later, keeps costs going longer, and can require Joby to raise more money. More stock issued would increase shares and could reduce value per share. My model shows the business is most sensitive to the revenue path, but I do not treat the per-share terminal-value output as a reliable valuation.

**Person B — sensitivity/interpretation:** Your revenue sensitivity has a larger impact than gross margin. Does that mean revenue is always the most important risk?

**Person A:** No. It is the largest driver only in the ranges I tested: a 30% revenue range and a 10-percentage-point margin range. Different ranges could change the ranking.

**Person B — teach-back:** Joby’s result mainly depends on how quickly commercial service grows. The biggest limitation is that the future revenue ramp is an assumption because the core air-taxi business is not yet established.

**Person B — feedback:** Strength: the model shows how slower revenue can lead to more funding and more shares. Improvement: find stronger evidence for certification date, fleet growth, fares, and utilization assumptions.

**Person A — decision:** Keep the linked sensitivity model. Investigate certification timing, production capacity, utilization, and pricing. Revise the revenue path if new evidence shows a slower commercial launch. The valuation conclusion remains limited because terminal value is not yet defensible.

### Joby FY2025 10-K evidence check

Source: [FY2025 Form 10-K](https://ir.jobyaviation.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001819848-26-000160/joby-20251231.htm), year ended December 31, 2025.

- **Business model (pp. 10, 37–39):** Joby is developing a piloted all-electric eVTOL air taxi; stated routes include Joby-operated service, affiliate-operated service, and direct sales/defense. The $75M-to-$3.5B path is therefore a scenario, not a mature-history extrapolation.
- **Certification/operations (p. 38):** Three of five FAA type-certification stages were completed or substantially completed, with stage four more than halfway complete. Joby also needs eVTOL operating-rule compliance and further authorizations. Certification is not the same as scaled passenger revenue.
- **Results (p. 41):** FY2025 revenue was $53.425M, R&D $581.101M, and net loss $929.842M. Reported revenue included Blade services, DOD operations, demonstrations, and engineering services; it does not establish a core eVTOL ramp.
- **Liquidity/share basis (p. 52):** Cash plus short-term investments were $1.408B and common shares outstanding were 915.077M. Any equity raise needs an issue-price and dilution assumption.

**Conclusion:** Keep revenue-path sensitivity, but investigate certification, production, approvals, fares, utilization, and contribution margin before relying on the 2030 scenario.

## NuScale Power — presentation summary

1. **Input:** Operating-cost growth alongside revenue growth; current revenue is engineering/licensing services rather than recurring module sales.
2. **Evidence boundary:** Binding contracts, commercial timing, cash obligations, and dilution remain research priorities.
3. **Model link:** FY2025 revenue was $31.479M and operating cash outflow was $459.610M. Revenue growth is tested at −10% / 0% / +10%; operating-cost growth at −2% / 0% / +2%.
4. **Base result:** 2030 EBIT is −$181.505M and FCFE is −$174.639M. No per-share sensitivity value is claimed because sustainable cash flow and a defensible going-concern valuation remain unresolved.
5. **Sensitivity:** Operating-cost growth gives a $41.510M FCFE span; revenue growth gives a $9.362M span.
6. **Interpretation:** Cost growth is the larger modeled driver only over the stated ranges.

### Person A reviews NuScale

**Person A — selection/evidence:** NuScale has some engineering and licensing revenue. Why do you not treat that as proof that module sales are starting?

**Person B:** The current revenue mostly comes from engineering and licensing work. It does not prove a recurring module-sales business. My forecast depends on real customer projects moving from planning into contracts, construction, and revenue.

**Person A — model/valuation:** How does a delayed project affect your model?

**Person B:** A delay pushes commercial revenue later while costs can continue. That makes cash burn worse and may lead to more financing or dilution. My model shows negative FCFE through 2030, so I do not claim a reliable per-share DCF value.

**Person A — sensitivity/interpretation:** Operating-cost growth has a larger sensitivity result than revenue growth. Is that definitely NuScale’s largest risk?

**Person B:** No. It is the largest driver only over the ranges I tested. The cost range is larger in dollar impact in this model. A different timing, module-sales, or project-contract range could change the ranking.

**Person A — teach-back:** NuScale’s result depends on commercial projects becoming real revenue-producing projects. The biggest limitation is that future contract timing and economics are uncertain, so the current model cannot support a strong per-share value.

**Person A — feedback:** Strength: the model keeps negative cash flow instead of hiding it. Improvement: research binding contracts, project timing, cash obligations, and potential dilution.

**Person B — decision:** Keep the signed FCFE sensitivity analysis. Investigate customer contracts, project timing, funding needs, and dilution. The valuation conclusion remains unresolved because sustainable commercial cash flows have not yet been established.

## Reflection

Sensitivity rankings are conditional on selected ranges and model structure. They are not probabilities, confidence intervals, or proof that one uncertainty is universally more important. Keep negative FCFE signed, do not manufacture a terminal value, separate historical revenue from the commercialization ramp, and label each launch-date, revenue, margin, financing, and share-count assumption as evidence-based or analyst judgment.
