# Project 1 — Edition A

## Decision, user, company, and valuation date

**Decision user:** A buy-side investment committee with no current position in Joby Aviation, Inc. (NYSE: `JOBY`).

**Decision:** Whether to initiate research coverage and advance Joby to a full FCFF/enterprise-value valuation, rather than whether to purchase the stock immediately.

**Company:** Joby Aviation, Inc. (`JOBY`; CIK 0001819848), an electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft developer and emerging air-mobility operator.

**Valuation date:** September 1, 2026. Financial statement reference period: fiscal year ended December 31, 2025.

## Initial thesis — watch / defer

**Watch / defer; do not initiate a position yet.** Joby is admissible for deeper research because it is an operating company with a meaningful reported service-revenue base and a large liquid balance-sheet resource relative to its present operating scale. However, a purchase decision is premature: 2025 revenue was driven by acquired and ancillary services while the core eVTOL business remains dependent on certification, commercial launch, manufacturing execution, customer adoption, and continued financing. The valuation will be highly sensitive to the date and scale of durable commercial cash flows, not just a near-term revenue multiple.

## Consequential assumptions

1. Joby can obtain the necessary certification and operating approvals on a timetable that permits commercial service before liquidity is materially impaired.
2. The company can manufacture aircraft and develop operating infrastructure at a cost and pace consistent with an eventual positive FCFF profile.
3. Passenger demand, route economics, utilization, pricing, and safety acceptance can support returns above the company’s cost of capital after launch.
4. Future capital raises, convertible-note conversion, warrants, earnouts, and equity compensation will not dilute per-share value beyond the economics assumed in a full model.
5. Cash and short-term investments are available as non-operating value in the bridge only to the extent they are not required to fund the operating plan.

## Unknowns and risks to resolve

- The certified commercial-launch date, market-by-market regulatory permissions, and probability of delay.
- The normalized revenue, margin, capital-expenditure, and working-capital profile of a scaled eVTOL service.
- Aircraft production rate, supply-chain reliability, and cost per aircraft.
- The portion of reported 2025 revenue and assets that is recurring and representative of the intended eVTOL operating model.
- The appropriate treatment and valuation impact of the 2032 convertible notes, warrants, earnout liabilities, lease liabilities, and other potentially dilutive securities.
- The required return, terminal assumptions, and scenario probabilities appropriate for an early-commercialization aerospace business.

## Research and validation plan

1. Build a dated capital-structure schedule from the 2025 10-K and subsequent 2026 SEC filings, separating period-end common shares, weighted-average diluted shares, options/RSUs/warrants, earnouts, and the 2032 convertible notes.
2. Reconcile cash, investments, debt, and debt-like claims to a single valuation date before calculating equity value.
3. Build base, delayed-certification, and accelerated-execution FCFF scenarios. Make launch timing, aircraft deliveries, utilization, price, contribution margin, R&D, and capital spending explicit drivers.
4. Validate certification and launch milestones against FAA/regulatory documentation and the company’s SEC disclosures, not against a search-result summary.
5. Separate acquired service revenue from the forecast economics of Joby’s intended eVTOL network; use a comparable-company and precedent-transaction framework only as a cross-check.
6. Run sensitivity tables for launch delay, production rate, utilization, fare/yield, margin, dilution, and discount rate; identify the inputs that move equity value per share most.
7. Complete the synthetic enterprise-to-equity bridge check using the class inputs and preserve the result alongside the model.
